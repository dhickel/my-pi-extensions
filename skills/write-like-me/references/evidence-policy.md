# Evidence Policy

Use this policy whenever the document contains factual claims, research, quotations, statistics, legal requirements, product behavior, comparative performance, or citations.

## Source Boundary

Classify each factual statement privately as one of the following:

1. **Supplied:** Explicitly present in material provided by the user.
2. **Verified:** Supported by a source whose relevant content was inspected.
3. **Derived:** Calculated from supplied or verified information, with the method made clear.
4. **Analytical:** A reasoned interpretation, comparison, or recommendation that does not pretend to be an external fact.

The writing corpus provides style evidence only. Its claims, references, quotations, statistics, and bibliography must not be reused as factual support for a new document.

## Claims Requiring Verification

Verify the following before presenting them as facts:

- quantities, percentages, dates, prices, and forecasts;
- laws, regulations, standards, and mandatory requirements;
- scientific or historical claims;
- attributed positions or findings;
- product features and current capabilities;
- comparative performance claims;
- quotations and page numbers;
- superlatives and claims of uniqueness;
- publication metadata, URLs, and digital object identifiers.

A bibliography entry does not prove that a source supports a claim. Inspect the relevant source content.

## Citation Rules

- Preserve the citation style requested by the user.
- Put the citation immediately after the clause or sentence it supports.
- Attach one citation to several claims only when the source supports all of them.
- Copy names, titles, dates, page numbers, URLs, and identifiers from verified metadata.
- Quote only wording that was inspected directly.
- Do not complete partial citation details from memory.
- Ensure every in-text citation has a corresponding reference entry.
- Ensure every reference is used unless the requested style permits a background bibliography.
- Do not invent a plausible source to support an otherwise reasonable statement.

## Revising User Material

Treat claims already present in user material as supplied content, not automatically verified truth. Preserve their intended meaning unless the user asks for fact checking. Do not add certainty, numerical precision, attribution, or citations from memory.

If the revision changes a factual claim materially, verify the new version or clearly identify it as analysis.

## Missing Support

When a required claim lacks support:

- inspect an authoritative source when research is permitted;
- ask for the source when the exact authority matters;
- omit unnecessary specificity;
- frame a proposed action as a recommendation rather than an established fact; or
- use `[SOURCE NEEDED]` only in a document explicitly labeled as a draft.

Do not leave placeholders in final output.

## Evidence in the Target Voice

Evidence should support a decision rather than decorate the document.

Use this order when it fits the material:

1. State the factual claim.
2. Place the citation beside it.
3. Explain the mechanism or practical significance.
4. Stop once the consequence is clear.

Do not repeat a statistic in several sections. Give it one canonical location and refer to the resulting decision only when later sections need it.
